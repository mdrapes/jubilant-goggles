A re-engagement prompt that forgot who I am

Surfaced from the home screen to a Silver-tier member with ten bookings and a list of saved shows (that's me!), TodayTix's personalisation prompt asks the user to hand-build a taste profile from a wall of unlabelled cards — ignoring everything it already knows — then offers no way out, and the prompt never appears again. Toggle between what's there now and a warmer fix.

The four fixes

Each maps to a way the current screen squanders a high-value moment.
1

Start warm — use what you already know

Lead with a few Collections chosen from the member's bookings and saved shows, each with the reason it's there. The ask becomes "confirm and refine," not "start from scratch."

Now: Ignores 10 bookings and every saved show — a loyal member is cold-started like a stranger.
2

Preview before you commit

A tap reveals what's inside a Collection, so a save is an informed choice.

Now: Collection names only — even a regular can't decode "Beat the clock" or "The 'Like a Londoner' Edit."
3

An exit that comes back

"Maybe later" lets the member leave — and quietly re-surfaces the prompt on next home screen load until it's activated or dismissed for good.

Now: No way out, and once you leave it vanishes and never returns.
4

A CTA that explains itself

A plain-language button that says where you stand and gives a status update on click.

Now: Greyed-out "Finish", no helper text, no indication of what's needed to proceed.

How this was made

The honest back-and-forth with AI tools — including where the AI was wrong and I had to steer.

  1. Started from friction I hit myself

    As a Silver-tier member, I tapped a banner on my own home screen to improve my recommendations — and ran straight into cards I couldn't decode, no way back, and a dead "Finish."

  2. The first AI pass got the framing wrong — usefully

    Working it through with Claude, its opening read was "confusing new-user onboarding." Reasonable, but it missed the thing that made my personal observations notable for loyal users.

  3. The reframe — the part only I could bring

    I'm not a new user; I'm a loyal one. Feeding in my tier, my ten bookings and the shows I'd already saved flipped the critique entirely: the real failure is cold-starting a member the app already understands.

  4. Pushed the data angle further

    Then noticed my saved shows also didn't affect the choices offered — and that the prompt vanished after a single view and never came back. A one-shot, unrecoverable moment.

  5. Prototyped the warm version

    Used Claude to build and iterate the before/after above — seeding suggestions from behaviour, preview-on-tap, an exit that returns, and a sensible CTA.

The takeaway: AI made this fast; the judgement about where it was wrong is what made it good. I treat AI's first pass as a draft to interrogate, and bring the context the model can't have.
